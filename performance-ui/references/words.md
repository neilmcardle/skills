# Words

Copy is part of the interface. These are the habits that make it read as written by a person.

## Capitalisation

**Sentence case.** Sentence case reads like speech and stays consistent when labels change. Title Case Turns Every Button Into A Headline.

Source: [Neil McArdle's rules](https://neilmcardle.com), Apple.

## Em dashes

**None.** Generated copy leans on em dashes. Full stops, commas and colons do the same work and read as your voice.

Source: [Neil McArdle's rules](https://neilmcardle.com).

## Labels above headings

**Never.** A small tracked label over a heading is the single most common generated-page habit. The heading can carry its own weight.

Source: [Neil McArdle's rules](https://neilmcardle.com), Impeccable.

## Button labels

**Specific verbs.** A button says what happens when you press it, and that name follows the action to the end. A button that says 'Book' should produce a confirmation that says 'Booked'.

Source: frontend-design.

## How errors speak

**Problem and fix.** Errors state the fault and its resolution, in the interface's voice. They do not apologise and they are not vague.

Source: frontend-design.

## Closing summary lines

**Stop when done.** A last sentence that restates the paragraph is a generated-copy habit. Stop when the point is made.

Source: [Neil McArdle's rules](https://neilmcardle.com).

## Joining details on one line

**Commas or space.** Strings of details joined with middle dots read as template chrome. Commas, or space between items, read as written.

Source: frontend-design.

## Who is speaking

**The product.** Say who is speaking and do not inflate it. A sole author writing 'we' sounds like someone hiding behind a staff that does not exist; a company writing 'I' sounds like nobody in particular. Most product copy can make the product the subject and sidestep the question.

Source: [neilmcardle.com](https://neilmcardle.com).
