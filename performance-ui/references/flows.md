# Flows and states

How a site behaves across a task: forms, errors, saving, paying, starting out.

## Form layout

**One column.** One column reads top to bottom with no guessing about order. Two columns save space but people miss fields and zig-zag.

Source: Baymard.

## Error messages

**Under the field, with the fix.** An error should sit next to the problem, say what went wrong and say how to fix it. A red outline alone tells nobody anything.

Source: frontend-design, Apple.

## Deleting and undoing

**Undo afterwards.** A confirmation on every delete trains people to click through it. Letting them undo is kinder and faster. Save confirmation for what cannot be undone.

Source: Apple.

## Checkout structure

**Three steps with progress.** Fewer steps means fewer places to leave. Merge steps that ask related questions, such as extras and protection, into one.

Source: Baymard.

## Saving

**Update now, roll back on failure.** If an action almost always succeeds, show the result at once and quietly roll back on the rare failure. Waiting for the server makes a fast app feel slow.

Source: Vercel, Apple.

## Prices that do not change at the end

**Total from the start.** Fees that appear at the last step are the most common reason people abandon a booking. Show the total people will pay from the first price they see.

Source: CMA guidance.

## First run

**Straight into the work.** The fastest way to show what a tool does is to let people do the thing. Tours explain a product people have not yet cared about.

Source: Impeccable, Brilliant.

## Search

**Results as you type.** Results that update as you type turn search into browsing. It needs fast data; otherwise search on submit and say so.

Put the search term in the URL.

Source: General practice.
